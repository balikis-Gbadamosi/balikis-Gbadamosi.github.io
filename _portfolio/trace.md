_In development · Research & design phase (2026)_
Many of the ways we cope are never named. They get called "attitude," or
"character," or simply "how someone is", and so the patterns left behind by
early adversity go untraced until they quietly compound into harm. This is
especially true in African and diaspora communities, where there is often no
shared language for these experiences at all. Trace is an interactive application
that helps people recognize and find language for the experiences and patterns that have shaped them. 
It sits deliberately before the clinical system, surfacing adversity-linked patterns
that are commonly normalized as "personality." The tool does not diagnose or
assign clinical labels; instead, it offers recognition, reflection, and
pathways to culturally appropriate support.

## What Trace Does

**Recognition & Mapping Layer**
Guides users through a brief, structured self-reflection — roughly twenty-four
questions across three sections: current stressors, patterns of coping and
relating, and early-life experiences. Using a transparent, non-clinical
classification framework, it maps responses to eight adaptation patterns —
such as people-pleasing, hypervigilance, emotional numbing, and family
patterns — and surfaces the one to three that resonate most strongly. Early-life
experiences act as an amplifying lens rather than a diagnosis, helping people
understand how present-day patterns may connect to earlier experiences across
the life course.

**Language Layer**
A glossary of grounded, accessible terms — organized beneath the same eight
patterns and rooted in African realities — gives users language for experiences
that frequently go unnamed, with clear compassionate understanding. 

**Support Layer**
Connects users to practical resources and to context-oriented therapeutic
support, addressing the stigma that often keeps people from seeking help
before patterns deepen. Every result is framed as a starting point for
reflection and, where helpful, professional support — never a verdict.

## Methodological Grounding
Trace integrates validated measures of childhood adversity and coping with an
original classification framework centered on African kinship systems and
intergenerational adversity. Responses feed a transparent, normalized scoring
model that assembles a personalized reflection from pre-defined pattern
components, rather than generated interpretation — keeping results consistent,
explainable, and safe. Its design is informed by the Stress Process Model,
Life Course Theory, and the Adverse Childhood Experiences literature.
